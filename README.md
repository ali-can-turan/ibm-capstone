### 🎯 Project Aim
The project aims to analyze popular coding environments and tools and to explore their relations to education levels, compensation, and job satisfaction.

### 💡 Key Insights
- $100K will continue to be the ballpark average for yearly compensation, with $250K being the virtual top **EXCLUDING** outliers.
- Job satisfaction will still **BE BASED ON** work experience, work modes, and work stress rather than compensation.
- JavaScript, Python, SQL, TypeScript, PostgreSQL, Amazon Web Services, Node.js, and React will be the leaders of the near future, not without threatening rivals. Go and Rust represent the **NEW WAVE** within programming languages.
- MySQL should be watched carefully in the coming years, as its decrease in popularity might **BECOME CHRONIC**.
- HTML/CSS will continue to lose ground as simpler frameworks allow developers to build **AESTHETIC** and interactive web pages/apps.
- **A UNIVERSITY DEGREE** will continue to bring more job opportunities, but often with an in-person work mode.

### 🔍 To Dwell On
- **The number of jobs** in Washington D.C. is 65% higher than in New York, **EVEN THGOUH** the latter is two and a half times more populated.
- C++ is one of four languages whose users earn more than $100K annually **DESPITE** its unpopularity, falling outside the top 10.
- Front-end developers earn 20% less compared to full-stack and back-end developers, **WHILE** they constitute **one-ninth** of the respondents across these three categories.
- High earners are usually 24-35 year-olds with a bachelor’s degree, **WHEREAS** the highest job satisfaction is reported by the unemployed.
- Swift is the language whose users earn the most on average, **BUT** the reason for this remains unclear.
- Rivalry between database brands **is overt**, **THOUGH** PostgreSQL seems unbothered as a leader.
